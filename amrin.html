From Scholarship to First Salary

For Amrin, a scholarship meant staying in college. Training helped her find her footing. And her first salary gave her something even more powerful, the confidence to dream of giving back.

Amrin Fathima Janofar’s proudest college moment came when she saw her name on her college banner. It announced that she had secured a job at Finzley in Chennai through campus placement.

“I felt really happy,” she recalls.

But the journey to that moment had not been easy.

During her first year studying Computer Science and Engineering at Government College of Engineering, Tirunelveli, Amrin lost her father. Life suddenly changed. Continuing her education meant placing an even greater financial burden on her mother, and she was unsure how she would manage.

“That’s when SSF came into my life,” says Amrin, who is from Tenkasi, Tamil Nadu.

Her faculty applied for the SSF scholarship on her behalf. After the selection process, Amrin was chosen.

“I felt like a huge weight had been lifted off my shoulders. I don’t know how I would have continued without this scholarship. It helped me focus on my studies and gave me a path forward.”

But for Amrin, SSF’s support went beyond financial assistance. She found the training particularly transformative. The sessions on aptitude, problem-solving and communication helped her approach challenges differently and prepared her for life in the corporate world.

“They taught me to look beyond the limits and approach a problem from a different perspective,” she says.

The weekend English and communication training also helped her become more confident. She remembers eagerly participating and answering questions throughout the three-and-a-half-month course.

Then came another milestone: her first salary.

“Tears of joy flooded my eyes when my first salary was credited to my account. Going from receiving an allowance from my mother to sending money to my family gave me a tremendous sense of pride and freedom.”

For Amrin, that first salary was more than a pay cheque. It was a symbol of how far she had come.

She credits the support she received through the scholarship with helping her reach this stage and already knows what she wants to do with that opportunity.

“Definitely, in the future, I want to pay it forward.”
